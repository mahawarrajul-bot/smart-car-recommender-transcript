● All five steps are written, but none of it has been compiled or run, because
  this machine has no Node, npm or tsc. I checked the data with a script: 50
  vehicles, 50 unique IDs, and every EV has kwhPer100Mi while every gas or
  hybrid has mpg. I also ported the scoring math to Python, and its rankings
  looked sensible. For example, a $30k budget in CA ranks the Corolla Hybrid
  first, then the Camry, Prius, Civic and Elantra Hybrid.

  I haven't written any tests. Once Node is installed, run tsc --noEmit first,
  then add Vitest tests.

  I added lib/engine/constants.ts, which wasn't in your spec. It holds the
  shared numbers: the ±20% window, the $10k–$50k limits, and per-state gas
  price, electricity price and insurance multiplier for all 50 states plus DC.

  Choices I made
  - Inputs: location is a two-letter state code. annualMiles defaults to 12,000
    and is optional. minReliability is on a 0–10 scale and defaults to 5.
  - Powertrains: I modelled gas, hybrid and electric. Plug-in hybrids are left
    out so the fuel math has no special cases.
  - calculateTCO(vehicle, location): annualMiles and the ownership years go into
    the TCOCalculator constructor. The ranker builds the calculator from the
    user's mileage. There is also a calculateBreakdown() that returns each cost
    component.
  - Scoring: each of the five factors is scored 0–100 and weighted
    25/25/20/20/10.
    - Budget: 100 at the cheap end of the window, 80 at your budget, 0 at +20%.
    - TCO: scaled across the candidates that passed the filters.
    - Emissions: combustion CO2 for gas and hybrids, average-grid CO2 for EVs.
  - Ranker and validator: rankVehicles expects input that has already passed
    validate(). An unknown state throws a RangeError.

  Limitations to check
  - Vehicle numbers are estimates. Prices, resale percentages and reliability
    scores are rounded from memory, not looked up. Check them against current
    MSRPs and EPA ratings before showing them to users as facts.
  - The ±20% budget window cuts off cheaper cars. A $30k budget excludes
    anything under $24k, which is how you wrote it, but it may not be what you
    want.
  - Some budgets return nothing.
    - $12k returns nothing, because the cheapest car is the $17.5k Versa.
    - $50k with trucks only also returns nothing, because every truck costs $39k
      or less.

  Decision for you: should the lower bound of the budget window go, so cheaper
  cars still show up? If so, it's a one-line change in passesFilters in
  ranker.ts.

  I didn't create package.json or tsconfig.json, and all imports are relative so
  no path aliases are needed.
